Streaming TV

What connected TV is, and why it fits a retailer

Connected TV (CTV) is television delivered over the internet: Prime Video, Fire TV and the other streaming apps people watch on their living room TV. Your commercial runs full-screen inside those shows, the same as broadcast TV, with three big differences.

What's differentWhat it means for the retailer
Chosen householdsYour spot goes only to the households we pick: people near your stores who are shopping for clothing, shoes, fragrance and gifts right now.
Store-level geographyEach store gets its own trade area, down to the ZIP code. A mall store and a street-front store each get a plan that fits that market.
Paid by the impressionYou pay only for ads delivered to the households you target. Nothing goes to people outside your markets.
Measured past the clickWebsite visits, online orders, store pickups and offline sales tied back to the households that saw the ad, plus foot traffic attribution.

The audience

Who we'd reach

These audiences come from Amazon Ads, built from what Amazon's own signed-in customers shop for and watch. Advertisers never see anyone's personal information; Amazon matches the ad to the audience on its side. Every audience is limited to the trade areas around your stores.

How to read the audiences. In-market (IM): customers whose recent shopping and browsing show they're actively shopping a category right now. Lifestyle (LS): customers whose ongoing shopping and viewing habits show a lasting interest or life stage, like following fashion or being a parent. Both come from Amazon's own customers, not outside data.

Foot traffic

From the living-room TV to the phone that walks in

For a retailer, the sale still happens in the store. Foot traffic attribution, included at every level, follows the commercial from the TV to the shopper who walks into one of your stores, and to what they spent.

1
Pick the storesChoose every location or the ones that matter most. We draw a boundary around each store.
2
They see the commercialYour spot plays on a connected TV in a household we targeted.
3
The household links to its devicesThe TV and the phones in that home share a household connection.
4
They walk inA phone with location sharing enters one of your stores' boundaries and counts as a visit.
5
We measure the revenueVisits are matched to your sales, so you see the dollars that came from TV households, store by store.

Hooking up the revenue

  1. Store boundaries. You confirm the stores, and our measurement partner maps each one and checks data coverage before launch.
  2. Website tag. Your web team adds one Amazon Ads tag to your website to count visits, online orders and store-pickup orders.
  3. Sales file. You share a secure weekly file of loyalty-member purchases: date, store, amount, and the member's email or phone. Contact details are scrambled (hashed) before they're uploaded, so no one can read them.
  4. The match. Inside Amazon Marketing Cloud, sales are matched to the households that saw the commercial and walked in.
  5. Revenue in your dashboard. You see TV-driven visits and revenue by store, audience and commercial.

Privacy No personal information is shared with the retailer, USA Connected TV or DynamiCard. Results are anonymous, aggregated and statistical.
